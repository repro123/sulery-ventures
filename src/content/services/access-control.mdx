## Access control for homes

Access control is not limited to large organisations.

Homeowners may want additional control over access to specific entrances or areas of their property.

Depending on the property and requirements, access control may be considered for:

- Main entrances
- Gates
- Staff entrances
- Home offices
- Restricted rooms
- Other selected access points

The appropriate system depends on the type of entrance and how access needs to be managed.

If you are considering access control for your home, tell us what you want to achieve and we can discuss the available options.

Businesses often need to control access to their premises while allowing authorised staff and visitors to enter.

An access control system can help businesses manage selected entry points without relying entirely on traditional keys.

Access control may be useful for:

- Office entrances
- Staff-only areas
- Server or equipment rooms
- Stores
- Restricted workspaces
- Business premises with controlled entry

Schools may need to manage access across different parts of their premises.

We can help schools and educational institutions discuss their access control requirements and determine a practical solution.

Institutions and larger organisations may have multiple buildings, entrances and restricted areas.

## Choosing the right access control system

There is no single access control system that is suitable for every property.

The appropriate solution depends on several factors.

### The type of entrance

Different doors, gates and entry points may require different locking and access control equipment.

### Number of users

A system for a small office with a few users may have different requirements from one used by a school or larger organisation.

### Number of access points

The number of doors or entrances that need controlled access affects the overall system.

### Preferred access method

You may prefer biometric access, cards, keypads or another supported method depending on your requirements.

### Security requirements

Different areas may require different levels of access control.

### Existing infrastructure

Existing doors, locks, power and network infrastructure may affect what can be installed and how the system is configured.

### Future requirements

It can also be useful to consider whether more users or access points may need to be added later.

Understanding these factors helps ensure that the system is appropriate for the property rather than simply choosing equipment based on appearance or price.

---

## Common access control methods

Different access control systems can use different methods to identify authorised users.

### Fingerprint access

Fingerprint-based systems can identify registered users through their fingerprints.

They can be useful where you want users to gain access without carrying a physical card or key.

### Access cards and tags

Cards or proximity tags can provide a convenient way for authorised users to gain entry.

They can also make it easier to manage access when users need to be added or removed from a system that supports this functionality.

### Keypad access

Users enter a configured code to gain access.

This can be a practical option for selected entrances where a shared or individually assigned access code is appropriate.

### Multiple authentication methods

Some systems may support more than one access method.

The appropriate option depends on the equipment and the requirements of the property.

## Managing access to different areas

Not every person in a business or institution necessarily needs access to every room.

An access control system can be used to restrict selected areas while leaving other parts of the premises accessible.

For example, a business may want general staff to access the main office but restrict access to:

- Server rooms
- Stores
- Management offices
- Equipment rooms
- Other sensitive areas

Similarly, a school may want to restrict access to administrative or staff-only areas.

The specific setup depends on the system and the requirements of the organisation.

## Why proper access control installation matters

Access control is not simply about putting a reader next to a door.

The reader, locking mechanism, power supply, door hardware and other components need to work together properly.

The system should also be configured around the way people actually use the property.

Poor planning or incorrect installation can result in unreliable access, unnecessary inconvenience or equipment that does not work as expected.

We focus on understanding the requirements of the property before determining the appropriate installation approach.
