## Choosing the right intercom system

There is no single intercom system that is suitable for every property.

The right system depends on several factors.

### Number of locations

A small home may only need communication between an entrance and one indoor station, while a larger property may require multiple stations.

### Audio or video requirements

Some properties may only require voice communication, while others may benefit from being able to see visitors.

### Entrance requirements

If the intercom will be used at a gate or entrance, additional equipment may be required depending on how access will be managed.

### Property layout

The physical layout of the property affects where intercom stations can be installed and how they can be connected.

### Number of users

A system for a single home is different from one that needs to serve multiple offices, apartments or departments.

### Existing infrastructure

Existing wiring, power and other infrastructure may affect the installation requirements.

Understanding these factors helps ensure that the system is selected based on how it will actually be used.

## Audio vs video intercom

One of the first decisions when choosing an intercom is whether you need audio communication only or both audio and video.

### Audio intercom

Audio intercom systems allow people to communicate using voice.

They can be a simple and practical solution where visual identification is not required.

### Video intercom

Video intercom systems provide both communication and visual information.

Depending on the equipment, a user may be able to see the person at an entrance while communicating with them.

Video intercoms can be particularly useful for homes, offices, schools and other properties where visitor identification is important.

The best option depends on your requirements and the type of property.

## Why proper intercom installation matters

An intercom system needs more than just the installation of individual devices.

The stations, wiring, power supply and other components need to work together correctly.

The system should also be designed around the actual layout and communication requirements of the property.

Poor installation or configuration can result in unreliable communication, audio problems or other issues.

Proper planning helps ensure that the installed system is practical and easy to use.
